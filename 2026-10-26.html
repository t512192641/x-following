Raycast 的设计天才之处，是用两个快捷键撑起了 2500 个扩展。

Enter 执行，Cmd+K 展开选项。就这两条规则，学一次，之后全部功能几乎不用重新学。这种设计力量，在产品圈很少见。

但最近录播客的时候，我用一个框架重新审视了 Raycast, 找到它的规则失效在哪里，那里通常就是产品结构的天花板。

答案是：AI。

Raycast 原本的节奏是「出现、操作、消失」，几秒钟，干净利落。但 AI 的节奏完全不同, 你需要持续对话、加文件、调语气、在上一轮基础上继续迭代。第一个回答只是起点，不是终点。

所以现在 Raycast 有了两扇门：Quick AI 住在命令栏里，AI Chat 住在独立窗口里。在开口问问题之前，你要先想清楚走哪扇。

一条请求怎么走。你在命令行把密钥写进钥匙串，值从 stdin 读，不进 argv。预设是一个具名作用域，等于基础 URL 加允许方法加整条路径正则。

agent 要访问 API 时调 proxy_http，报预设名和路径；mcp-warden 先拿请求去过允许清单，过了才从钥匙串取密钥、注入、发往上游，只把响应还给 agent。全部跑在本机，Rust 写，Apache-2.0。

所以提示注入能做的事变少了。它可以把结果带出去，但抄不出一串自己没见过的 token。会话记录贴出来，也没有密钥可漏。

安全边界落在预设上，钥匙串只负责存放。README 自己指出的坑：预设不写 --allowed-paths 就等于放开 base_url 下所有路径，配经典 PAT 时 agent 能读这个 token 看得见的每个私有库。路径规则按整条路径锚定，不做前缀匹配，所以 ssh 那条写成 git pull.* 就等于放行 git pull; rm -rf /。

审批和审计是配套的。默认策略是 prompt，要设 auto 必须在建预设时显式加 --confirm-auto。macOS 和 Windows 的桌面通知只负责提醒，真正的批准发生在 stderr 里的 TUI 弹窗。每个动作落进本机 SQLite，审计接口没有 delete / clear。按窗口放行的调用记成 windowed，事后能看出哪几条没让你再点一次。

我的判断：它防的是提示注入骗 agent 把密钥发出去，agent 自己去翻钥匙串它不管。威胁模型 N2 写明，agent 没被沙箱隔离时，可以起子进程跑 security find-generic-password。所以它是凭证边界，要真隔离，还得把 agent 关进容器或虚拟机。另一处窄口是响应不做脱敏，放行 GET /me 就等于把邮箱这类信息交给 agent。

要上手的话，preset scaffold 里有 8 个内置模板，或者用 preset from-openapi 从 OpenAPI 3.x 规格生成一个。先只配读操作，把批准窗口留在 10 分钟以内，隔几天跑一次 mcp-warden audit，比一次配齐写权限稳妥。

My biggest takeaways from Rob Snyder, author of The Power of Pull:

1. Solving a pain point isn’t enough to get people to buy your product. Rob’s first startup checked all the classic boxes: hair-on-fire problem, a differentiated solution, and design partners using it daily. Yet he still spent two years pivoting aimlessly. Every prospect agreed it solved real problems, but none bought.

2. Rob’s "PULL" framework identifies four conditions that drive a buying decision: The buyer has a Project on their to-do list, and a reason it’s Unavoidable right now. They also have a List of options they’ve considered, each with Limitations that leave them stuck. That person is a “coiled spring,” desperate to find a product to buy.

3. The right cold outreach message needs to be interesting, not persuasive. Its only job is to get someone to take the call. Rob calls this “founder magic” outreach. One startup’s entire message was: “Hey, Snoop Dogg invested in us, and I thought you’d find it interesting too. Would you be open to chat?” No pain points, no value propositions, no personalization. Just something that gets a conversation. Rob’s LinkedIn benchmarks are 20 requests a day, 30% to 40% accepted, 30% to 50% of those replying, and most saying yes.

4. Design partners are a trap. Nearly every time, the partner buys into your vision, hands you a pile of feedback, and never buys. Then you realize “all of their feedback was just like them playing Steve Jobs.” The people who have something important to get done don’t want to be design partners. They just want it done. Offering a partnership is “a negative signal to people who actually have pull, and a positive signal to people who don’t.”

5. Pull belongs to a specific person, not to a company. A common founder mistake is to say “we sell to SpaceX” or “we sell to mid-market private equity firms.” In practice, you’re selling to the head of legal at SpaceX, who has a particular project on her to-do list she can’t solve with her current team. The company doesn’t have pull. She does.

6. The signal of real pull in a meeting isn’t just enthusiasm—it’s someone leaning in and asking how to move forward. “Wow, that’s amazing, totally the future of our industry, I can see how that would save us a ton of money” is not pull. That’s a polite exit. What Rob looks for is a “pull-shaped action”: “Wait, you can do that? How do we get started? Does the product exist yet?” Before the product even existed, someone in pharma told one founder, “If you can do that, I will pay you right now.”

7. Run the first sales call like a detective, not a pitcher. Rob’s opening question is always some version of “I’m sure you get a lot of people reaching out. Why did you take this call?” The best-case outcome: they fill out the PULL framework for you unprompted. They tell you exactly what they’re trying to do, why now, and what they’ve already tried.

8. When a call goes lukewarm, throw the Hail Mary. Ask, “Is there anything on your to-do list that you would pay me to do right now?” A team selling applicant tracking software to governments got a lukewarm reaction but then learned the buyer would pay for help with job descriptions. That became their first contract. When founders are truly stuck, Rob recommends going into “goblin mode,” a burst of slightly crazy effort to get in front of customers, like the founder whose parents went door to door to dental offices for him.

9. Rob calls "The Case Study Factory" his most important idea. The job is to find one type of customer who says 'hell yes' before and after buying, then repeat that success through pipeline, sales, and delivery. This customer forms your repeatable case study. A startup finds one repeatable case study and then repeats it a million times. To grow as fast as possible, borrow the theory of constraints from manufacturing: find the bottleneck in the process of repeating that case study and treat it as the only thing that matters until it’s fixed.

A delivery orchestration platform that supplies FedEx estimated it would take 7 to 8 months to rebuild its core billing system.

We rebuilt the core in 3.5 months with 2 engineers, merging 122 pull requests in the first 90 days. The system was in production by month 6.

The core was ready in roughly half the estimated time. Getting it into production took longer.

The old system was a no-code tool that had grown into their billing engine. It invoiced shippers, paid carriers and handled disputes, but it was slow. Many of its rules weren't documented anywhere. We had to work them out from production data.

Their engineering teams usually had 5 to 10 people. Our pod had 2 full-stack engineers, supported by an AI productivity lead and a part-time PM. It was also the first AI Velocity Pod we'd ever run.

Three things made the difference.

1. Context before code.

For the first two weeks, nobody wrote feature code. We scanned the repo and built a knowledge graph covering every module, data flow and domain term. Some of the work involved building new code, and some connecting to systems other teams already depended on. The agents read that map before working on either.

2. The agent asked before it built.

Every feature started with a written spec, even as requirements changed daily. Before planning one feature, the agent asked how the system would know which costs belonged to the shipper and which belonged to the carrier. Nothing in the code answered that question. Resolving it during planning is straightforward. Discovering it on a live invoice means dealing with a dispute.

3. Engineers designed and reviewed. The agent typed.

The agent wrote about 90% of the code. Nothing merged unless our engineer could verify it, explain it and debug it without the agent. The client's principal engineer also reviewed every PR. Of the first 122, 104 had fewer than 5 reviewer comments.

Then came the rollout.

A small group of logistics providers tested it in October. It went live in December, and the old system was switched off early this year.

We merged about as many PRs in the two months after go-live as in the four months before it.

AI compute cost about $200 per developer per month.

Their CTO called our pod the "top performing team" inside the company. Both engineers received discretionary bonuses, twice. The client then hired more of our engineers.

Next time a portco brings you a 7 to 8 month estimate, ask how much of it is discovery nobody has done yet.

magpie 10-26 网关故障：尸检报告和道歉

10 月 25 日到 26 日下午，很多朋友更新 magpie 后遇到网关「挂了」：Codex 等 agent 的请求一直转圈，2 分钟后报 504（held this request for 2m0s without routing it），设置里关掉本地模型的开关也没反应。这是我的问题。耽误了大家的工作，尤其是半夜还在帮我抓栈、找原因的朋友，真的对不起。

【影响】 - 时间：10-25 起明显加重，到 10-26 14:15 的 v0.1.1566 修复。 - 范围：打开了「设置 › 隐私 › 用本地模型查找」的用户。重启 magpie 后几乎必犯，而且时好时坏。

【根因（已由用户抓到的 goroutine 栈确认，#3682）】 本地脱敏模型（pf-serve）的调用拿着一把全局锁做同步 I/O：模型加载（最长 3 分钟）和每一次识别都在这把锁里。于是所有请求都排在它后面，连「选哪个账号」这一步都走不到；设置页读模型状态、关闭模型也要这把锁，所以开关也点不动。

【为什么 10-25 起突然变严重】 - v0.1.1539 换了新版 pf-serve（pf-v2）。老用户更新后，magpie 会在后台下载新版、结束旧进程，下一个请求就要在锁里冷启动一次。我推断这一步把问题放大了，但没有单独复现过。 - v0.1.1540 加了「路由前卡住 2 分钟就回 504」的看门狗，原来静默的等待变成了直接报错。 - 识别超时后会结束并重启 pf-serve，每次重启又是一轮持锁加载，所以故障反复出现。 - 报告者的日志里，10-16 到 10-24 路由前等超过 5 分钟的请求是 0 条，10-25 有 26 条。

【我哪里做错了】 1. 把可能要几分钟的 I/O 放在全局锁里，测试和评审都没拦住。测试用的是假 pf-serve，从没用真模型跑过「冷重启 + 多个请求同时进来」。 2. 一天发了二十多个版本。换 pf-serve 这种「更新后第一个请求必然冷启动」的改动，没在真机上把更新流程走一遍就发了。 3. 10-26 凌晨 5 点就有朋友报告，我第一反应是「没复现，请先抓栈」。可抓栈的接口本身也会被卡住的请求挡住，直到 v0.1.1559 才修好。13:08 GitHub 上的 yunwang2442-creator 在 #3682 贴出完整的栈和根因，我才在一小时后修好。非常感谢。 4. 还没解释清楚的：有朋友报告 /v1/models 和供应商页面也没响应，这两条路径不经过脱敏模型，这次的修复不一定覆盖。turingou 的情况我也还没拿到栈，不能确定是同一个原因。在拿到证据之前，我不会说它已经修好。

这两年，同样的问题又发生在 AI 新闻上。

**看完这条信息，我能做点什么？**
