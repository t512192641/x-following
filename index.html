Out of everything posted about FSD v15 so far, one line from @elonmusk deserves way more attention than it's getting: "This is real-time AI video generation (no gaming engine!) using just half of one AI4 TRIP accelerator. Let that sink in …" 🔥

Half of one accelerator is the part worth sitting with. Once you see the numbers behind it, Reality View stops looking like a fancy new screen and starts looking like one of the most impressive things Tesla has ever shipped.

🎬 THE SCREEN IS NOW GENERATED LIVE

For years, FSD's screen worked like a video game. Software took a list of what the car detected and dropped stock 3D models in the right spots: a gray car here, a lane line there.

Reality View tosses that playbook out the window. The car's AI now generates the video itself, frame after frame, as you drive, with no game engine anywhere in the loop.

Better yet, the picture comes from the same end-to-end model that's doing the driving. That's the single neural network that turns camera video into steering and braking, and now it paints your screen, too.

⚙️ WHAT A TRIP ACTUALLY IS

The standard AI4 computer carries two self-driving chips, and each chip packs three custom neural processors Tesla calls TRIP accelerators. That's six in all.

And that's where Reality View gets made: right on the self-driving computer, instead of the infotainment chip that runs your maps and music.

Each TRIP is basically an arena packed with tiny calculators, all cranking through the multiply-and-add math that neural networks run on. Teardowns count 9,216 of them per TRIP, lined up in a 96-by-96 grid and firing 2.2 billion times a second.

Each calculator handles two operations per tick, so one TRIP works out to 9,216 × 2 × 2.2 billion, or roughly 40 trillion operations every second.

Half of one comes to about 20 trillion, roughly a twelfth of the whole computer's neural muscle.

📏 WHY THAT NUMBER IS RIDICULOUS

Twenty trillion operations per second sounds enormous, until you see what else runs on that kind of budget.

The Neural Engine inside Apple's iPhone 15 Pro is rated at 35 trillion operations per second. So on paper, the slice of compute painting Reality View has less raw AI muscle than the AI chip in a 2023 phone.

Companies don't all count these numbers the same way, so it's a ballpark, but it shows just how little compute this is.

At the other end of the scale sits Nvidia's H100, one of the data-center workhorses powering today's AI boom. It's rated at about 1,979 trillion 8-bit operations per second in its standard mode, the same kind of math a TRIP runs, which puts half a TRIP at roughly 1% of one H100.

That gap matters because AI video is one of the toughest jobs in AI. Many AI video generators still take minutes to produce a single clip, as the researchers behind the real-time system MotionStream point out.

Even MotionStream, built specifically for speed, streams roughly 17 to 29 frames per second at 480p, and about 10 at 720p, while eating up an entire H100.

Meanwhile, Tesla is streaming a live, photorealistic view of the real road on roughly 1% of that chip's rated power, inside a moving car.

In other words, Reality View runs on less compute than a phone's AI chip, inside a computer whose main job is driving.

There's a fair asterisk here. Those research models dream up whole scenes from a prompt or a single image, while Tesla's renderer starts with a driving brain that already knows exactly what's around the car. Tesla also hasn't shared the view's resolution or frame rate.

And even on the most generous estimates of AI4's power, half a TRIP still works out to only about 2% of one H100.

Either way, live generated video on a sliver of a car computer would have sounded like science fiction not long ago.

🔍 HOW IT CAN BE THIS CHEAP

Tesla hasn't spelled out how it works, but it has left plenty of breadcrumbs.

FSD's network has been building its own 3D picture of the world for a while now, something Tesla showed off at the ICCV conference in October 2025. That picture includes 3D Gaussians, millions of soft little blobs of color that can rebuild a scene you can view from any angle.

Tesla also built its own Gaussian splatting system that can rebuild a scene from the car's cameras in a fraction of a second.

Reality View streams its picture from a virtual camera that can sit anywhere above the car, and showing a scene from an angle no real camera has is exactly what those 3D blobs are great at.

Even Elon seems to like that theory: he called a description of Reality View as a neural, Gaussian-splat digital twin "pretty accurate", and it lines up with every other clue.

If that's the trick, the hard part is done before the screen ever needs a picture. The driving brain understands the scene, and the visualization just paints what it already knows.

That would explain the other shocker. Reality View swapped every separate signal behind the old screen for one end-to-end visualization, and it still uses less compute than the bare-bones 2020 visualization did.

A view this cheap to generate is the perfect canvas for that kind of shared knowledge.

Elon asked everyone to let that sink in, and the math earns it: half of one accelerator in a $650 computer is painting the real world live while FSD drives.

Lots of founders are crashing out. A year ago AI felt like an incredible opportunity.

I run a 200-person engineering company, and I feel some of this too. I expected AI to change how fast we could build. I underestimated how much it would change the job for our engineers and what customers would expect from us.

1. Reviewing code all day is tiring.

AI writes about 98% of the code we ship. We can generate changes faster than our engineers can review them. When you write something yourself, you know why you made each decision. When you review an agent’s work, you have to piece that together before you can decide whether to ship it.

Some of our engineers love working this way. Others miss having a few uninterrupted hours to build something themselves.

2. How do junior engineers learn to debug now?

A small bug makes a good first ticket. You trace it through code you’ve never touched, and by the time you fix it, you know how that part of the system works. An agent can close the ticket before you’ve figured out where to look.

Anthropic had 52 developers learn an unfamiliar Python library. On a quiz right afterward, the group with AI averaged 50%, compared with 67% for the group that coded by hand. The biggest gap was in debugging.

3. If AI writes the code, why hire an engineering partner?

Sometimes the customer can build it themselves. But many of our prospects said they were short of people who could build what they needed. One put it plainly. “We're not engineers by background and we aren't interested in being engineers either.”

On a logistics rebuild we did, the customer’s old no-code tool had become their billing system. Before we could replace it, we had to work out which billing rules people still relied on and how to switch over without interrupting invoicing. That’s a fair answer to what the customer was paying us for.

4. What happened after launch?

Many of our clients had already had a bad experience with a consultancy, agency or vendor. A dozen said they struggled to get their own people to use AI. Those are two things I’d ask an engineering partner about. Show me something you shipped a year ago. Who uses it today? What went wrong, and who fixed it?

We’ve run a performance marketing platform since 2023. At one point our own QA process was holding up releases, and we raised it with the customer. If I were hiring us, I’d want to hear that part of the story too.

Small teams and one workflow at a time are working for us.

The questions I’m paying more attention to now are who gets stuck reviewing everything, whether the next engineer gets enough practice to share that load, and whether the customer’s team is still using what we built once we’ve moved on to the next release.

一个小 bug 是很好的第一个任务。你在从未接触过的代码中追踪它，等你修复它时，你就了解了系统的那部分是如何运作的。代理可以在你弄清楚从哪里查找之前就关闭任务。

有时客户可以自己构建。但我们许多潜在客户表示，他们缺少能构建所需产品的人。其中一位直言不讳地说：“我们不是工程背景出身，也不感兴趣成为工程师。”

我们从 2023 年起运营一个绩效营销平台。有一次，我们自己的 QA 流程拖慢了发布，我们向客户提起了这件事。如果我是雇主，我也想听到故事的这一部分。

小团队和一次一个工作流程对我们有效。
