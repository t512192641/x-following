用模型权重反推训练用的是哪种 GPU？原理拆解一下👇

核心不是训练留下的痕迹，而是「初始化指纹」。

1️⃣ 随机数是确定的，但排布取决于硬件 PyTorch 在 CUDA 上用 Philox（计数器式伪随机数生成器）生成初始权重：给定 seed、线程序号和偏移，输出就是确定的。初始化 kernel 的线程数按 GPU 的 SM 数量来算，每个线程按跨步循环往张量里填数。 所以第 i 个权重拿到的是哪个随机数，取决于 SM 数。按原帖的说法，Hopper 是 132 个 SM，Blackwell 是 148 个，Grace Blackwell 是 152 个。 同一个 seed 换一种芯片，初始权重的排列就完全不同。（SM 数, seed）就是一枚硬件指纹。

2️⃣ 训练抹不掉初始值 从没出现过的 token、padding 对应的 embedding 行几乎没被更新过，可能和初始值逐位一致。其余参数每一步的更新相对初始范数都很小，训练完和初值仍高度相关。

3️⃣ 暴力搜索 + 统计检验 原帖枚举了约 6 万亿组「芯片布局 × seed」。Philox 能直接算出任意位置的值，不用生成整个张量，每个假设只算几千个采样点，和公开权重算相关性就行。 巧合匹配最高约 8σ，真实匹配在 49σ 到 1385σ 之间。之后还在 B300 上把 DeepSeek-V4-Flash 的初始权重逐位复现出来，换成 H100 或 seed 改 1 就对不上。

4️⃣ 边界 • 只能证明「初始化」在哪种芯片上跑，理论上初始化和训练可以不在同一个集群 • 前提是直接在 GPU 上用 PyTorch 默认生成器初始化。改成 CPU 初始化、换 RNG、做置换或加噪声，指纹就消失了。公开之后，各家实验室很容易把它抹掉 • 不能说明芯片在哪里

思路非常巧妙：把工程上「可复现」的设计，反过来变成了取证手段。等论文和代码放出来，值得复现一下。 https://x.com/willdepue/status/2120992457630326841

A founder putting together a roll-up of trades businesses told our team about a pitch he keeps hearing from other roll-ups: "we're going to build our own operating system, everything we touch is going to be our own." He said some investors like it.

His view: whoever builds it has to maintain it, and think about security and uptime for as long as it runs. I agree with him, and we're a company that gets paid to build software.

In a services roll-up, the CRM, accounting, payroll and dispatch are solved problems. If you build your own, you also sign up for maintenance, security, uptime and an engineering team that wasn't in anyone's model.

Say you plan to buy eight companies over three years and you build your own platform first. The first acquisition can't move onto it until it exists, and that usually takes longer than anyone planned. Every company after that waits on the platform team's roadmap. And the platform team becomes a permanent line in the P&L.

Where a roll-up can actually be different is narrower than an operating system. Usually it's two or three things: how fast you integrate an acquired company, how you quote and price, and the reporting the board actually reads.

What I'd do:

1. If you're starting the roll-up now, pick one off-the-shelf system per function and move each acquisition onto it as you buy it. Boring, and fast. 2. If you already own ten companies on ten systems, I wouldn't stop everything to migrate. Build around what's there. 3. Build only the layer that makes you different, on top of those systems. That's also where AI tends to be worth it. 4. Before you build anything, write down who owns it and what it costs to run each year.

The messy part: sometimes the off-the-shelf system really doesn't fit, and building is the right call. In my view that's usually one piece of the stack, not the whole operating system.

If a roll-up's deck says it built its own operating system, I'd ask how many engineers keep it running and what happens when one of them leaves.
