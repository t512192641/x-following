A PE operating partner brought us into a portfolio company that runs outsourced revenue cycle management for about 20 community hospitals.

The CEO wanted three production AI agents live in 90 days, while its 40-person engineering team was already committed to a platform migration.

We narrowed the scope to technical and administrative denial appeals and built the first working agent in six weeks with one senior AI engineer, supported by a fractional AI lead and part-time delivery support.

Before we started, 12 denial specialists prepared appeals manually alongside their other responsibilities. They interpreted denial codes from 835 remittance files, retrieved supporting documentation, and rewrote payer-specific cover letters.

The company had roughly 4,000 open technical and administrative denials with its top five commercial payers, some approaching their appeal deadlines.

We agreed to start with appeals instead of eligibility verification or underpayment detection because the data was more accessible and the financial value easier to measure.

HIPAA-compliant handling of patient data was a requirement from the outset, so we signed a subcontractor BAA before accessing claims data, used BAA-covered model hosting, and built PHI redaction into logging from day one.

The first two weeks went into mapping the workflow, reviewing six months of claims data, and establishing a baseline.

写增长的子木 @CoderJeffLee

你的 GEO 竞品，不一定是产品竞品。

真正需要研究的，是 AI 为回答目标问题搜到了谁，最后又引用了谁。

一份 fan-out 报告可以把这两层分开：

第一层是 Sources。

这些页面出现在 Web Search 返回的来源中，说明它们与模型展开后的某个搜索需求相关。

第二层是 Citations。

这些页面最终出现在答案引用里，是更值得优先拆解的内容样本。

在一次 best ai ppt maker 测试中：

1 次 Web Search 调用； 展开出 4 条搜索词； 返回 41 个来源 URL； 最终答案引用了 3 个 URL。

这 41 个来源，是可扩展的 GEO 竞品池。

这 3 个引用，则是优先级更高的研究对象。

接下来逐页检查：

它是什么页面类型？ 回答了哪个具体问题？ 用了什么数据、案例或产品证据？ 内容结构有什么不同？ 它为什么能同时匹配搜索词和最终回答？

GEO 竞品研究，不该只列几个熟悉品牌。

应该从真实问题出发，找到反复出现在搜索来源和答案引用里的页面。

你更想先拆“被搜到但没引用”的页面，还是“最终被引用”的页面？

生成这份报告的开源 Skill 放在第一条评论。

Engineers stay on the product whose stack they know. In his words: “A person that does tax will stay in tax.”

He’s right, with one condition. His engineers told him the agents couldn’t understand the codebase.

That’s the default in a roll-up: every acquired system has its own conventions, and they live in a few people’s heads. An agent with no map is a guessing machine.

Here’s the order I’d run it in: 1. Map each acquired codebase first: structure, conventions, what calls what. That map is what lets an agent work in code nobody on the team wrote. 2. Pick one product family whose team already measures delivery, so you have a baseline. 3. Let engineers work outside their stack on that product, with agents doing the heavy reading and a senior reviewing every PR. 4. Reorganize around products once the numbers make sense.

That $15 million is locked inside codebases the agents can’t read yet.

Map it first. Then let the agents go get it.
