A head of innovation at a PE-backed fund services firm told me what they

Alex Prompter @alex_prompter

A head of innovation at a PE-backed fund services firm told me what they found in their first month on the job.

Contractors had come in and built AI tools fast. "Vibe coding," in their words. A lot of money went out. What came back was some basic HR automation and, as they put it, "nothing that's production ready."

Their verdict on the contractor spend: "It has not gone well."

We've seen this on a lot of calls this fall. A demo takes a few days now, so a vendor can show progress for months without anything going live.

If you've inherited a pile of AI work like this, four questions will sort it:

1. Where does it run? If the answer is somebody's laptop or personal account, you have a prototype.

2. Who gets the call when it breaks during month-end? If you can't name the person, nobody owns it.

3. What do you test it against? Without a set of correct answers your own team signed off on, you can't tell whether it still works after the next model update.

4. Which number moved? Cost per case, days to close, cash collected. If nobody can name one, you paid for a demo.

This person has more ideas than they can count. They asked me for a few things to get shipped, and in my view that's the right ask.

Taking what somebody already built and making it a reputable, governed, stable thing is a big part of our work.
