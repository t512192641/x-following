MCP Events 定稿进入 Beta：用订阅和游标把外部事件叫醒 agent

yibie @yibie

MCP Events 让 agent 被外部事件叫醒

MCP 原本是请求驱动的：模型问一句，服务器答一句。11 月，Events 扩展的提案 SEP-3415 定稿为 Final，扩展进入 Beta。它让客户端订阅上游系统里发生的事，新邮件、代码推送、生产事故都能在没人提问时触发一个 agent turn。

已有的 resources/updated 这类通知干不了这件事：它只说某个 URI 变了，没有订阅参数和载荷；只在打开的连接上到达，断线就丢；也没有游标，客户端不知道自己错过了什么。

Events 的流程是：服务器在 server/discover 里声明能力，客户端用 events/list 拿到事件目录，再带参数订阅，比如只订某个服务的 P1 事故。事件以 occurrence 到达，包含 eventId、timestamp、data、cursor；客户端每收一条存下 cursor，重连后拿它重订，服务器补不到就回 truncated: true，客户端就知道中间跳过了。

投递有三种模式，没有一种强制。poll 按服务器建议的间隔拉取，服务器不留状态，适合 serverless；push 挂一个长请求推通知，适合本地或常在线的客户端；webhook 让客户端登记 https 回调，服务器把 HMAC 签名的事件 POST 过来，适合守不住连接的远程客户端。SDK 一般按 webhook、push、poll 的顺序选。

客户端跟进得快但参差：截至 11 月 19 日，MCPJam 和 Mastra 三种都支持；ChatGPT 只支持 webhook，且基于定稿前草案，限 Work chats 和 dots；Vercel AI SDK、TanStack AI 也只有 webhook；Claude Code 还是个开放的功能需求。

安全上有一条值得记住：事件载荷是不可信数据，可能携带 prompt injection，规范要求当成工具结果一样清洗或沙箱处理，服务器只发够分诊的最小信息；订阅和投递时都要查权限，收回授权就终止订阅。

附言：如果是等自己发起的长操作（CI、人工审批）跑完，该用兄弟扩展 Tasks，拿持久的 taskId 轮询；外部系统主动发生的事才归 Events。

Alex Prompter：医疗场景里 AI 演示最容易垮掉的 8 个口

Alex Prompter @alex_prompter

Healthcare is where AI demos fall apart. These are the 8 places they kept breaking on our calls this autumn, several of them with PE-backed groups:

1. Every payer does it differently. A credentialing software CTO told us he could build an AI demo for any one payer's enrollment portal in seconds, and it would be useless in production. The forms change by provider type, specialty and line of business, even inside the same payer. Each task is easy on its own. There are just thousands of them, and every one the AI gets wrong negatively affects revenue.

2. Getting a doctor approved takes about six months per hospital. We asked the CFO of a PE-backed telehealth group who runs it. About five people, a light credentialing tool, and Excel sheets passed around by email. Some hospitals only meet once a month to grant privileges, so the AI would spend most of its time waiting on a committee date buried in somebody's inbox.

3. The schedule is a jigsaw. A doctor working 7 shifts has to email in 10 dates, and then the contract rules sit on top, like one holiday a quarter and one weekend a month. They tried moving it to a form and it didn't stick. So whatever you build ends up reading loose emails and guessing at rules that live in contracts it never sees.

4. The payer's rule shows up after the work is done. A surgeon told us he had about 20 unpaid bills in one month. Why? Well, the reason only appeared when the bill went in, instead of when the procedure was booked. You can't automate a rule that doesn't exist yet when the work happens.

5. The AI scribe doesn't read the chart. His patients come in with around 200 prior notes, and the scribe reads none of them. The note it writes sounds great, I mean it's fluent, but it's missing the history that matters.

6. The bots are priced per run. A billing company's CTO walked us through a seven-figure annual quote for unattended bots, charged every time one runs and each built on its own with no shared context. So the more claims you push through, the more you pay, and the bot still breaks the moment a payer moves a button on their website.

7. The billing logic is buried in the database. One PE-backed healthcare software company runs its core on about 10,000 stored procedures across 2,000 repos, and nobody can tell you for sure what each one does. Practically, that means AI shouldn't go anywhere near the code that bills a visit until somebody can.

8. More code, no visible value. Their CFO was pretty blunt about it. AI tools meant a lot more pull requests opened and closed, and he still couldn't see what any of it was worth to the business.

Every one of these is a payer rule, an inbox, a spreadsheet or an old database. That's why the demo always looks better than the first month in production.
