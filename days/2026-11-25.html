Expert acquisition guidance for SaaS founders

Andrew Gazdecki @agazdecki

Expert acquisition guidance for SaaS founders

Your startup might be worth a lot more to a competitor than it is to you.

This is one of the more interesting things about acquisitions.

Say you own a SaaS company with 2,000 paying customers and $300K in annual profit.

You know how much money the business makes, how fast it's growing, and roughly what it costs to acquire a customer.

But a competitor looking to acquire your business is doing some completely different math.

They already have a customer support team.

They already have a sales team.

They could potentially eliminate $100K in overlapping annual expenses.

And if just 20% of your customers buy another one of their products at $100 a month, that's another $480K in annual revenue.

Suddenly, your little SaaS company looks like a much bigger financial opportunity to them.

This is why I think founders should be careful about valuing their startups purely on a multiple of profit.

Every buyer has a different reason for wanting to acquire your business.

Some want the cash flow.

Others want your technology, your customers, or a faster way to enter a new market.

And occasionally, someone comes along who can make considerably more money from your business than you can.

That's also why having multiple buyers interested in your startup is so important.

You never really know who's going to see the most value in what you've built until you start having those conversations.

Everyone's hitting a wall with agentic engineering right now.

You don't hear much about it, partly because the people selling agents profit from the "solution". We sell agentic engineering too, so weigh this however you like.

The wall: agents can't tell if the thing is right for the people who use it.

Say an agent rebuilds a claims screen for a billing team. Every test passes. The team opens it on Monday, and the field they check first on every claim is now two clicks down. Nothing is broken, so no agent flags it. Repeat that across a few hundred PRs and you end up with a system that works and that nobody wants to use.

This wall is structural. It's why you can't just throw a pile of agents at a real business system and let them run. Someone has to keep stopping them to steer.

If you let agents run without that, the codebase slowly fills up with things that pass the tests and don't fit the work.

You can dodge the wall on problems with no people in them. Most of what our customers need is software their own staff use all day, so we don't get to.

And if you see someone claiming a fully automated software factory with thousands of agents, don't feel behind. Small teams ship. Ask who reviews the PRs.

OpenAI 称矩阵乘法理论最快是 9/4 次方；铁锤人提醒这不会让明天的显卡变快

宝玉 @dotey

宝玉补充：OpenAI 11 月 24 日在 GitHub 数学仓库放出一篇 111 页预印本《矩阵乘法指数是四分之九》，声称两个 n×n 矩阵相乘，运算量增长最快也就是 n 的 2.25 次方。他写明：2.25 的上限是 OpenAI 10 月初的旧结果，之后几组人类数学家已独立重写证明；这次补的是下限，目前只有 OpenAI 自己的论文和 Lean 证明，没有同行评审，也还没查到专家对下限的公开评价。这个结论管的是一种特定的算法计数规则（只数加减乘除、输入当代数符号、输出必须严格相等），不管数字有多少位、内存怎么搬，也不管近似计算。因此它管不到 AI 里常用的低精度量化、低秩近似；GPU 实际用的一直是课本算法，最多加一两层 Strassen，因为更快的算法要在大得离谱的矩阵上才划算。10 月上限从 2.371 降到 2.25 后，Houxi Lü 以及 Amanov、van den Berg、Zuiddam 把它推广到所有数域，Amir Shpilka 给出了可以写出来的具体算法。下限用反证法：如果有算法比 2.25 再快一点点，就能把「检查几组列表有没有重复元素」做得便宜到不可能。Lean 证明的是「不用除法」的算法，论文主定理还允许除法，范围比 Lean 宽一点。OpenAI 把仓库稿件称为「原始研究数据」，并提醒没形式化的结果可能有错，10 月 7 日曾因符号错误撤回 3 篇。

铁锤人也写了同一条新闻：模型把矩阵乘法的理论速度极限摸到了 9/4 次方，再快一步数学上做不到。他提醒这不等于明天显卡就会提速——好比去楼下 500 米买菜，坐飞机理论速度最快，光安检登机的时间，走路早就买完了。日常用 AI，眼前不会有变化。

Sam Altman 今日确认 9/4 已是紧的，并补充：由 Yin Tat Lee 等旧结果，这也意味着线性规划可在 n^{9/4} 时间内求解。
